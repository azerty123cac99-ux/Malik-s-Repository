Case 5 · Amazon · 2023

Warehouse Workers & Safety Reporting

  • Workers and labor advocates raised concerns about injuries, fast production quotas, and work pace.
  • OSHA cited several Amazon warehouses (2022–23) for ergonomic hazards; a 2024 Senate HELP Committee report criticized injury rates. Amazon disputes these findings.
  • Workers faced a choice: report hazards, or stay silent to protect hours, evaluations, and jobs.
Type of ethical issue
Nonrole

The conflict comes from personal circumstances, not a job duty: an individual weighing coworker safety against their own income. Frameworks: rights-based (right to a safe workplace) and utilitarian (fewer injuries overall).

Brand

5
Case 5 · Amazon · 2023

Possible solutions

01

Anonymous, no-retaliation reporting

Third-party hotline and written anti-retaliation policy; safety data reported to the board.

02

Safety-adjusted metrics

Remove injury reports from productivity scoring; slow rates when hazards are flagged.

03

Independent safety audits

External ergonomic audits with public results and correction deadlines.

Our best solution

Anonymous reporting plus safety-adjusted metrics. It removes the forced choice between safety and employment, which is the root of the dilemma, and it is fast and cheap to start.

Case 6 · Wells Fargo · 2023–2024

Unauthorized Accounts Scandal

  • Employees opened accounts and enrolled customers in products without permission to hit aggressive sales targets.
  • Regulators (CFPB, OCC, Federal Reserve, DOJ, SEC) fined the bank billions; in 2018 the Fed capped its asset growth until reforms were made.
  • Thousands of employees were fired; customers paid fees and suffered credit damage.
Type of ethical issue
Role Distortion

The job is to serve customers with suitable products. Quotas and bonuses redefined “good service” as “accounts sold.” Frameworks: duty-based (honesty, consent) and justice ethics (customers bore the harm).

Case 6 · Wells Fargo · 2023–2024

Possible solutions

01

Redesign incentives

Replace quotas with satisfaction, retention, and quality metrics; claw back bonuses for misconduct.

02

Verify customer consent

Digital consent for every new account; audit unusual account-opening patterns.

03

Protect ethics-line reporters

Independent hotline reporting to the board with enforced anti-retaliation.

Our best solution

Redesign the incentives. The misconduct came from the reward system, so controls and hotlines alone only treat symptoms. Pair it with digital consent checks.

Case 7 · Boeing · 2024

Whistleblowers & Safety Concerns

  • Employees such as John Barnett and Sam Salehpour raised concerns about manufacturing and quality control.
  • After the January 2024 Alaska Airlines door-plug blowout, scrutiny grew. Barnett died in March 2024 (ruled a suicide) before finishing testimony in his retaliation case.
  • Whistleblowers testified to the Senate; the FAA and Congress investigated.
Type of ethical issue
Role Assertion

Employees acted beyond their job descriptions to protect passengers, risking careers, legal costs, and well-being. Frameworks: duty-based (protect the public) and virtue ethics (courage, integrity).

Case 7 · Boeing · 2024

Possible solutions

01

Independent speak-up system

Confidential channel to a board safety committee with tracked responses and deadlines.

02

Enforced anti-retaliation

Fast, neutral review of claims; discipline managers who retaliate; legal support for reporters.

03

Safety in executive pay

Tie bonuses to quality and safety indicators, not just deliveries.

Our best solution

An independent speak-up system with enforced anti-retaliation protection. Credible internal reporting means employees needn't go to regulators or media first, and structured review screens out unsupported claims.

Case 8 · Meta · 2024

Employees & AI Projects

  • Tech employees, including at Meta, voiced concerns about AI misinformation, discrimination, privacy, and misuse of generative AI.
  • Examples: using public posts to train AI, which drew European regulator attention in 2024, and internal AI chatbot guidelines criticized in 2025.
  • Individuals had to choose: keep working, request safeguards, raise concerns, transfer, or leave.
Type of ethical issue
Nonrole

Employees rarely make the final call; the conflict is personal conscience versus contributing to a project with both benefits and risks. Frameworks: virtue ethics (integrity) and utilitarian (benefits vs. harms).

Case 8 · Meta · 2024

Possible solutions

01

Internal AI ethics review

Cross-functional board must approve high-risk launches with documented risk assessments.

02

Conscientious objection policy

Employees can transfer off objectionable projects without penalty.

03

Transparent safety reporting

Publish model cards, red-team results, and known limits.

Our best solution

An internal AI ethics review with a conscientious-objection option. It gives employees a real voice inside the company, which does more than leaving, and reduces harm before launch.
